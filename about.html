Simplification & Mental Models Retention Multiplier: 3.8x

Explain It Simply or You Don't Understand It

Named after Nobel laureate Richard Feynman, this technique forces you to teach a concept in the simplest possible terms without technical jargon. When you encounter a point where you cannot simplify, you have located your exact knowledge gap.

Execution Protocol:

  1. Choose the CS concept (e.g., QuickSort partitioning or B-Trees).
  2. Write a brief explanation using analogies understandable to a beginner.
  3. Pinpoint where you stumbled or used filler words.
  4. Review reference documentation to rebuild that specific link.

Memory Decay Neutralization Retention Multiplier: 4.5x

Hacking the Ebbinghaus Forgetting Curve

German psychologist Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered that memories deteriorate exponentially within 48 hours of initial exposure. By reviewing material at calculated intervals—just before you are about to forget it—the neural synaptic connections are hardened permanently.

Optimal Interval Schedule:

  • Session 0: Initial deep lecture and understanding.
  • Review 1 (24 Hours Later): 10-minute active recall drill.
  • Review 2 (3 Days Later): Problem set and edge case evaluation.
  • Review 3 (10 Days Later): Quick conceptual summary.

Effortful Retrieval Retention Multiplier: 5.0x

The Blurting Method

Reading notes creates familiarity, which the brain misinterprets as mastery. The Blurting Method requires you to close all books and tabs, take a blank sheet or editor, and write down every single formula, theorem, and code snippet from memory.

Color Coding Feedback:

Use green ink for concepts remembered accurately, and red ink for items you missed. Focus your next 25-minute Pomodoro exclusively on the red items.

Cognitive Discrimination Transfer Multiplier: 2.9x

Interleaving vs. Blocked Practice

Solving 20 identical array problems in a row (blocked practice) teaches pattern memorization, not problem-solving. Interleaving mixes disparate problem types (e.g., one graph traversal, one dynamic programming, one hash map problem) forcing the brain to first diagnose which tool to apply.

Exam Day Superiority:

Interleaved learners consistently score 43% higher on unexpected exam questions because they have practiced classification, not just automated execution.
