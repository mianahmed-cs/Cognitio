Evidence-Based Study Techniques

Cognitio is grounded in established cognitive psychology. Click through the proven study methodologies below:

1. The Feynman Technique (Learn by Teaching)

Named after Nobel Prize-winning physicist Richard Feynman, this technique proves whether you truly understand a concept or merely memorized its definition.

Step 1: Choose a Topic

Pick a difficult computer science topic (e.g. recursion or binary search trees) on a blank page.

Step 2: Explain Simply

Explain the idea as if teaching an 8-year-old using simple words and no jargon.

Step 3: Spot Your Gaps

Whenever you get stuck or use vague terms, return to your textbook to clarify.

Step 4: Refine and Simplify

Use relatable analogies to solidify the logic until you can explain it effortlessly.

2. Active Recall (Retrieval Practice)

Passive reading produces an illusion of competence. Testing yourself before looking at the answers forces neural pathways to strengthen permanently.

Passive Reviewing (Low Efficiency)

Re-reading highlighted slides multiple times feels easy but leads to rapid forgetting during exams.

Active Retrieval (High Efficiency)

Close your notes and write out the algorithm from memory. Struggle strengthens memory retention by over 300%.

3. Spaced Repetition (The Forgetting Curve)

Hermann Ebbinghaus discovered that memories fade exponentially unless reviewed at calculated expanding intervals.

Day 1

Initial lecture study and summary creation.

Day 3

Quick 10-minute active recall session.

Day 7 & 21

Permanent retention review before midterms.

Study Habits of High Performers

Deep Work Sessions

Isolate your study sprints from social media and instant messages. 25 minutes of single-tasking yields greater cognitive retention than 2 hours of multitasking.

Interleaved Practice

Instead of studying only one topic for 6 hours straight, alternate between theoretical problem-solving and programming exercises to keep your brain engaged.
